Tracks & data

There are ten tracks, all on real-world public data. Each team answers its track's core question and adds at least 2 sub-questions of its own. Kaggle downloads need a free Kaggle login.

T1 · Air quality × weather

How much of the variation in PM2.5 comes from weather, how much from season, and how much from the 2020 lockdown?

Data
CPCB Air Quality Data in India (city_day.csv) + Open-Meteo Historical Weather API
Join
City + date
Hard part
Weather has to be pulled from the API for every city, and many pollutant values are missing.
T2 · Rainfall × crop yield

Does monsoon rainfall drive kharif crop yield, and which states hold up when rainfall changes?

Data
Rainfall in India 1901–2015 + Crop Production in India
Join
State + year
Hard part
Rainfall is recorded by meteorological subdivision but crops by district, so you must build a subdivision-to-state mapping.
T3 · Bengaluru housing × restaurants

Does the number and rating of restaurants in a locality explain its price per sq ft?

Data
Bengaluru House Price Data + Zomato Bangalore Restaurants (~550 MB)
Join
Locality name
Hard part
Locality names differ between the two sources and need fuzzy matching. total_sqft contains ranges and mixed units.
T4 · COVID-19 × development

Which development indicators go with lower COVID-19 death rates?

Data
Our World in Data COVID-19 (compact CSV) + World Bank WDI
Join
ISO3 country code + year
Hard part
Daily data must be aggregated to years, aggregate regions are mixed in with countries, and reporting has gaps.
T5 · Happiness × economy

Beyond GDP, what separates the happiest countries from the least happy?

Data
World Happiness Report (2015–2019 files) + World Bank WDI
Join
Country + year
Hard part
Column names change from year to year, and country names must be matched to ISO3 codes.
T6 · Bike demand in two cities

Do the effects of weather and time of day on bike demand carry over from one city to another?

Data
Bike Sharing, Washington DC (UCI) + Seoul Bike Sharing Demand (UCI)
Join
Stack both into one common schema
Hard part
DC temperatures are normalised while Seoul's are in °C, and seasons and holidays are coded differently.
T7 · US flight delays

Which airlines, airports and time slots cause the most delay, and do delays carry over through the day?

Data
2015 Flight Delays and Cancellations (flights + airlines + airports)
Join
Airline code, airport code
Hard part
With 5.8 million rows you need a sampling strategy. Times are stored as HHMM integers, and some airport codes are numbers.
T8 · IPL 2008–2024

How has scoring changed in each phase of the innings, and does winning the toss matter?

Data
IPL Complete Dataset (matches + deliveries)
Join
match_id
Hard part
Ball-by-ball data must be rolled up to matches, teams were renamed over the years, and some matches have no result.
T9 · Olympics × wealth

Do richer and more populous countries win more medals per person, and do host countries have an advantage?

Data
120 Years of Olympic History (athlete_events + noc_regions) + World Bank WDI
Join
NOC → region → ISO3 + year
Hard part
A team medal must be counted once, not once per athlete. Historical NOCs such as URS and GDR need mapping.
T10 · Used-car depreciation

What decides a used car's resale value, and how fast do cars lose value by brand and fuel type?

Data
Vehicle Dataset from CarDekho (merge all of its files)
Join
Name + year + km driven
Hard part
The files have different layouts, units are written into the text ("23.4 kmpl", "1248 CC"), and the same car appears in several files.

Our World in Data stopped updating its GitHub COVID-19 repository in August 2024, so use the compact CSV linked above. For World Bank data, use DataBank downloads or the wbgapi Python package, and name every indicator code you use (for example NY.GDP.PCAP.CD for GDP per capita).

Mandatory technical requirements

All nine stages are required. For each one, show the code, its output, and a written explanation of why you chose that method.

  1. Problem framing

    • Write a one-paragraph problem statement and name who would use your answer.
    • State at least 4 hypotheses as H0/H1, each with the test you plan to use.

  2. Data acquisition and integration

    • Download or fetch every source with code, not by hand. For API sources, save the fetch script.
    • Build the join key, using mapping tables, name matching, or fuzzy matching where needed.
    • Write a join audit: row counts before and after each merge, the list of unmatched keys, and why you chose that join type.

  3. Data quality audit

    • Present it as a table: feature × issue × count × action.
    • Cover data types, exact and near duplicates, inconsistent categories and units, and impossible values.
    • For missing values, give the percentage per feature, a missingness heatmap, and a reasoned judgement of MCAR, MAR or MNAR for the top 3 features.

  4. Missing values

    • Compare at least 2 imputation methods on the same feature (for example median vs KNN, or group-wise vs interpolation for time series), and show how each changes the distribution.

  5. Outliers

    • Detect outliers with at least 2 methods (IQR, z-score or modified z-score, Isolation Forest, or domain rules), compare the results, and explain whether you keep, cap or remove each one.

  6. Exploratory and statistical analysis

    • Use univariate, bivariate and multivariate visuals suited to each feature type.
    • Run a formal test for every hypothesis (t-test or Mann-Whitney, ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis, chi-square, Pearson or Spearman). Check its assumptions and report the effect size, not only the p-value.
    • For the time-series tracks (T1, T2, T4, T6, T8), decompose trend and seasonality.

  7. Transformation and feature engineering

    • Show skewness before and after a log, Box-Cox or Yeo-Johnson transformation, and explain your scaling and encoding choices.
    • Create at least 5 new features, each with a one-line reason.

  8. Feature selection and dimensionality reduction

    • Use correlation analysis plus VIF or mutual information to remove redundant features.
    • Run PCA and show a scree plot and cumulative explained variance, then interpret the top 2 components.

  9. Insight and communication

    • Build a one-page dashboard, static or interactive with Plotly or Streamlit, that answers the core question.
    • Give at least 3 recommendations a stakeholder could act on, each backed by a specific chart or test result.
    • Include a limitations section covering bias, confounders, and what the data cannot tell you.

Advanced extensions are optional and count only toward the top-3 ranking: clustering with profiled segments, a geospatial map, an interactive Streamlit app, or a sensitivity check showing your conclusions hold under a different cleaning choice.

Rules

  • Participation is optional, and no session has an attendance requirement.
  • Teams have 3–4 members, and each student can be in only one team. At most 2 teams can take the same track, and they must work on different sub-questions.
